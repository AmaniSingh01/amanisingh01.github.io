Problem framing

What enters the model

Two delay types are modelled

Operational primary delay
Ground staff · crew · technical
→
Reactionary delay
Inherited from previous flights

Weather, security, ATC, passenger and other factors are represented as a single lumped exogenous constant. Operational delay is partly endogenous; reactionary delay is endogenous through propagation.

The model narrows the problem to controllable operational primary delay and the way it multiplies through the schedule. It does not attempt to model every cause of flight delay.

Sensitivity analysis

±10% parameter runs

What moves average delay most?

Recovery fraction

Dominant and sharply asymmetric: a 10% decrease adds 15.7 min; a 10% increase removes 6.8 min.

Propagation strength

Second most influential; a 10% increase adds 7.5 min, increasing runaway-loop risk.

Approximately 3.0 min either way; the enlarged exogenous bucket outranks the buffer.

Management implications

Recommendations from the model

1 · Prioritise recovery capacity

Fix the binding constraint first.

A 20% lift in recovery cuts delay from 17.5 to 10.8 minutes and is estimated in the model to save €60M/year. The suggested focus is turnaround crews, spare aircraft and gate slack.

2 · Improve ground processes selectively

The four ground factors matter, but are secondary. Each factor moves delay by less than 0.25% per 1%; baggage is the largest. The model estimates a 25% programme at approximately €24M in value.

3 · Set buffers deliberately

The buffer cost curve indicates a cost-optimal point near 19.7 minutes, with approximately €23M saving. However, the curve omits throughput loss from longer turnarounds, so the true optimum would be lower.

4 · Sequence investment

Combined recovery and buffer changes give the lowest delay (9.3 min, 80% OTP), but cost more than recovery alone. The recommended sequence is recovery first, then buffer only if a tighter delay target requires it.

Limitations & further work

Interpreting the model honestly
  • This is a calibrated base-case scenario, not an optimised solution; no formal cost-minimisation was run.
  • The buffer cost curve omits schedule throughput lost to longer turnarounds.
  • Misconnection and rebooking remain at zero at 17.5 minutes because delay is below the 30-minute connection buffer; they activate only under disruption.
  • Sub-factor weights are proportional estimates; drop-one-out contributions overlap through the feedback loop and are marginal, not additive.
  • Recovery and propagation parameters are calibrated to CODA aggregates, not independently measured. Propagation strength is the least-sourced influential parameter.
  • The model represents one aggregated airport / aircraft class, without network spill-over or weather stochasticity in the base case.
What would strengthen the analysis: independently measure recovery and propagation from airport operations logs; add throughput opportunity cost; stress-test passenger outcomes under a disruption day; split the exogenous bucket into weather, ATC and security; and run formal cumulative-cost optimisation before claiming an optimum.
