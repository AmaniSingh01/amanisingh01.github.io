How the analysis was built

Research workflow
01 / FRAME

Started with roughly 50 candidate variables and applied a strict pre-release information rule.

02 / OPERATIONALISE

Narrowed to measurable variables, using explicit coding rules for star power, director form, social relevance and VFX dependence.

03 / PREPARE

Cleaned the wide-release dataset, treated missingness carefully and created a stratified train / validation / test split.

04 / MODEL

Fitted and iteratively trimmed a nominal logistic regression in JMP Pro; compared model specifications.

Key finding
Films succeed on people — but the model explains only part of the story.

Top-actor status had the strongest statistical signal, followed by social relevance and the director's recent track record. Top-actress status and VFX-heavy positioning were also significant in the primary model.

The model is statistically significant, but most variation in flop / not-flop outcomes remains unexplained by the measured pre-release variables.

This is an association-based model, not proof of causation and not a guaranteed hit predictor. Word of mouth, competition, timing and luck remain outside the pre-release inputs.

What did not hold up?

Null findings matter

In the primary model, remake / adaptation status, censor certification and music-director fame were not individually significant after controlling for the other terms. The report also notes that trailer-view and buzz proxies were dropped because of substantial missingness or weak operational reliability.

Scope & limitations

Read with context
  • Trade verdicts are used as the proxy for commercial success.
  • Only wide theatrical releases are included; limited and niche releases are outside scope.
  • Star and music rankings are year-specific and involve judgement at the margin.
  • Findings are associations, not causal effects, and apply to the Indian wide-release market in the study period.
  • The primary model's pseudo-R² is modest; box-office outcomes remain noisy.
