Does emotional storytelling shift attention away from the product?

A 2 × 2 experimental study testing whether metaphor-led advertising changes what people look at and how they respond — and whether the effect differs between a familiar brand and an unfamiliar one.

The familiarity manipulation worked. However, the 2 × 2 ANOVA found no significant ad-type × brand-fame interaction for purchase intent (p = .719). The metaphor advantage did not detectably differ between the two brands.

What the analysis suggests

Interpretation

Curiosity did not explain the effect

A bootstrap mediation test with 5,000 resamples found no evidence that curiosity mediated the effect of ad type on purchase intent or brand attitude. The indirect-effect confidence intervals included zero.

The curiosity scale had low internal consistency (alpha = .38), and the sample was small. The mediation result should therefore be interpreted cautiously.

The central takeaway

A metaphor ad may sell the moment without necessarily selling the brand name.

Emotional storytelling increased togetherness and stated purchase intent in this sample. It did not produce a statistically significant change in brand attitude or curiosity.

Limitations & next steps

Research quality
  • Small valid sample (n = 30), with unequal groups and a smallest condition group of five.
  • The purchase-intent result (p = .037) is fragile because several tests were run without correction.
  • Rivo Cola resembled Coca-Cola; 7 of 25 respondents exposed to Rivo mentioned Coca-Cola or a copy.
  • 26 of 50 eye-tracking recordings were flagged for weaker calibration; gaze findings are descriptive.
  • Each respondent saw one ad for six seconds, and outcomes were self-reported rather than observed purchases.
  • Further work: larger sample, more distinct unfamiliar brand, stronger calibration, more reliable curiosity scale and a real choice measure.
